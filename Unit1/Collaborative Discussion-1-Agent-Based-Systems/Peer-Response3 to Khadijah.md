I agree that AI agents are becoming increasingly important across organizational functions. Your comparison of an AI agent to an employee raises an important question about how organizations should redesign work when humans and agents contribute to the same processes.

Rather than viewing agents mainly as employee replacements, organizations could distribute tasks according to capability. Routine and data-intensive activities may be delegated to agents, while humans retain responsibility for judgement, creativity, relationships, and accountability. KPMG (n.d.) similarly argues that workforce planning should increasingly integrate human and digital workers.

Effective agent performance also depends on how organizational knowledge is represented. Russell and Norvig (2021, Chapter 8) explain how first-order logic can represent objects, relationships, and facts, enabling intelligent systems to reason about their environments. PwC (n.d.) further suggests that organizations should redesign roles and team structures rather than defaulting to workforce reduction.

Do you think organizations should redesign existing roles around human and agent collaboration rather than deciding which jobs agents could replace?
