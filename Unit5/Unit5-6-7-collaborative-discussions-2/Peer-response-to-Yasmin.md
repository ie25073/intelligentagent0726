# Peer Response to Yasmin Almeida Ferrera

Yasmin, thank you for extending my discussion to the challenge of scale. Your point suggests that my proposed hybrid architecture needs a clearer account of how agents establish and maintain shared meaning as participants and vocabularies change.

Payne and Tamma (2014) provide a useful foundation through their Correspondence Inclusion Dialogue, which allows agents to negotiate ontological mappings while selectively disclosing private knowledge. However, their model explicitly involves two agents. I would therefore be cautious about treating its results as evidence that the same approach scales across a large network. Agreement between two participants does not establish that their mappings remain compatible with those negotiated elsewhere.

To address this, I would propose reusing validated mappings associated with specific ontology versions, with renegotiation triggered by vocabulary changes or detected misunderstandings. For example, a purchasing agent and a logistics agent should establish whether “available” means physically in stock or available for immediate dispatch. Reusing that agreement could reduce repeated negotiation, but only while its assumptions remain valid. KQML facilitators can help agents locate suitable services (Finin et al., 1994); semantic compatibility would still need separate validation.

Your feedback therefore strengthens my hybrid proposal: ordinary methods would handle stable internal operations, while ACL interactions would include explicit semantic agreements and defined failure responses. I would evaluate this design using message volume, negotiation time and successful task completion as agent numbers increase.

Would you favour a shared core ontology with negotiated extensions, or local ontologies aligned only when agents need to interact?
