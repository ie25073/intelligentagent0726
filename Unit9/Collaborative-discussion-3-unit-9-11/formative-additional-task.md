# Additional Task: Preventing fabricated evidence in an Academic Research Agent

The safety issue I investigated is the generation of unsupported claims and fabricated references by an intelligent research agent. NIST (2024) describes confabulation as confidently presented false or erroneous content and recommends verifying sources and citations. I learned that an apparently reasoned explanation can itself be unreliable; confidence and detail are insufficient evidence of correctness.

Walters and Wilder (2023) provide empirical support. Across 636 citations from 84 generated literature reviews, 55% of GPT-3.5 citations and 18% of GPT-4 citations were fabricated. The study concerns particular models and prompts used in 2023, so these figures cannot predict our proposed system's performance. Nevertheless, they establish a concrete risk that requires testing.

This matters for our Group C Academic Research Agent because unsupported evidence could pass between agents and acquire apparent credibility through repetition. A researcher might then waste time locating nonexistent publications or base conclusions on claims that the cited studies never made. Such failures threaten academic integrity and trust.

I would address this through explicit checks at each stage. The retrieval stage would retain publication identifiers, metadata, source locations and retrieved passages. The synthesis stage would connect substantive claims to those passages and clearly distinguish reported findings from interpretation. References would be assembled from retrieved metadata rather than generated from memory. A verification stage would check both publication existence and whether the source supports the claim. Unavailable evidence would trigger an explicit limitation or human review, never an invented replacement.

Before deployment, I would evaluate a manually checked collection of research questions, including missing sources and contradictory findings. Measures would include citation validity, claim support and appropriate abstention. Human approval would precede dissemination, supported by an inspectable evidence record. These are proposed controls, not implemented results. Retrieval and additional agents can still fail, so I would monitor errors and repeat evaluation after changing models or data sources.
