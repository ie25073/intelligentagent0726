# Initial Post

Deep Learning technologies such as DALL-E and ChatGPT create important ethical concerns because they can generate realistic images and fluent text that may appear trustworthy even when the content is inaccurate, biased or misleading. Their ability to create new content offers clear benefits for education, research and creativity, but it also creates risks that require careful review.

One concern is reliability. Walters and Wilder (2023) found that GPT-generated academic references could include fabricated citations, showing that convincing language does not guarantee factual accuracy. This is especially important in academic contexts, where users may rely on generated information without checking the original source.

Bias is another concern. Deep Learning models learn patterns from large datasets, and those datasets may contain social or cultural biases. As a result, generated content can reproduce stereotypes or disadvantage underrepresented groups. NIST (2024) also identifies harmful bias, privacy, information integrity and over-reliance on AI as important generative AI risks.

There are also issues around consent, authorship and misuse. Generated images or text can be used for impersonation, misinformation or imitation of creative work. I therefore believe these systems should be supported by safeguards such as source verification, bias testing, privacy protection, clear disclosure of AI-generated content and meaningful human oversight.

How can these safeguards be implemented without reducing the usefulness and creativity of generative AI systems?
